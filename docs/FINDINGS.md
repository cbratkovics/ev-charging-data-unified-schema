**Period.** Boulder, 2018-01-01 to 2023-11-30, non-trivial sessions at known stations.

**What was found.** Over 14,742,434 connected minutes, 6,643,229 were idle after charging ended: an idle share of 45.1%. <!-- cite: artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.connected_minutes; artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.idle_minutes; artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.idle_share_of_connected --> Of those idle minutes, up to 1,834,372 elapsed while every inferred port at the station was occupied (idle at full occupancy): up to 12.4% of connected time, or 27.6% of idle time. <!-- cite: artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.full_occupancy_idle_minutes; artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.full_occupancy_idle_share_of_connected; artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.full_occupancy_share_of_idle --> This is an upper bound on displaced demand, for three reasons: ports are lower bounds, so a station with an uncounted port was not full; at a station with one inferred port every idle minute counts as full occupancy by definition; and no source records whether anyone was waiting. **Why it matters.** The headline idle share is the number that usually gets quoted as recoverable capacity. On this data it is 3.6 times the full-occupancy figure, and even the smaller figure is a ceiling: most idle minutes happen while another port at the same station is free, and the rest may or may not have kept anyone waiting. <!-- cite: artifacts/findings/latest.json#boulder_idle.production.idle_to_full_occupancy_ratio -->

**What I would tell the decision-maker.** Quote the full-occupancy figure as "up to", never the headline. If an idle fee or a time limit is worth trying, target the late-morning-to-mid-afternoon hours at the multi-port stations, where the measure carries information; overnight idle at a single-port station displaces nobody the data can see.

**What would change my mind.** Queue or turned-away-driver records would turn the ceiling into an estimate; an inventory of ports would remove the lower-bound caveat and could lower the figure; evidence that charging starts later than plug-in would move minutes from idle to charging.

The 17,677 accepted rows that do not meet the stated rule are ordinary sessions the publisher excluded for reasons it did not state. <!-- cite: artifacts/findings/latest.json#dft_anomalies_population.accepted_not_meeting_rule --> The 9,258 duplicates whose surviving twin is in the fasts raw file are the fast-charger events the revision moved between publications. <!-- cite: artifacts/findings/latest.json#dft_anomalies_population.moved_to_fasts_raw --> In the other direction, 274 rows of the rapids raw file meet the exclusion rule yet were published as clean. <!-- cite: artifacts/findings/latest.json#dft_publisher_rule.by_family.rapids.meets_rule -->

**Why it matters.** Anyone who reproduces the publisher's figures from the raw file and the stated rule will not get the publisher's numbers, and anyone who trusts the anomalies file as 'the bad rows' will discard thousands of ordinary sessions.

**What I would tell the decision-maker.** Treat the publisher's split as a label, not a rule: keep every row, carry the label as a flag, and apply one explicit session rule of your own (here: energy above zero and more than three minutes) so the exclusion is reproducible.

**What would change my mind.** A published methodology note listing the other exclusion criteria (incomplete records, events from other schemes) would explain the unexplained rows; the reports mention such criteria without counts.

**Why it matters.** A utilization figure quoted without its denominator definition is not comparable with any other, and the swing here is larger than most year-on-year changes an operator would act on.

**What would change my mind.** An inventory of ports per station would replace the inference and collapse the range.
